What is HerHealth? 💗

HerHealth is your all-in-one menstrual wellness companion — a soft little corner of the internet that helps you understand your body instead of fighting it. It brings together a smart Period Tracker that learns from your logged cycles and predicts what comes next, an interactive dashboard with your cycle ring, calendar and hormone chart, and a cosy self-care tips library covering food, movement, myths and mindful breathing for every phase. No judgement, no scary medical jargon — just gentle guidance, honest information and a reminder that every phase of your cycle has its own kind of strength. 🌷

Care for every phase 🌈

Your needs change through the month — here's how to honour each season.

🌧

Menstrual · Day 1–5

Rest is productive now. Curl up with a hot water bottle, sip warm drinks like ginger or chamomile tea, take slow walks if you feel like it, and let yourself cancel plans without guilt. Warmth soothes cramps — warm baths and heat pads are your best friends.

🌱

Follicular · Day 6–14

Fresh energy is blooming! Fill your plate with fresh, colourful food, try new recipes, start that project you've been dreaming about and say yes to social plans. Your body is rebuilding — protein and iron help it along.

💕

Ovulation · Day 14–17

You're glowing — confidence peaks around ovulation. Antioxidant-rich foods like berries, leafy greens and nuts support this sparkly phase. Schedule the presentation, the date, the photoshoot — this is your main-character moment.

🌙

Luteal · Day 18–28

Slow down and reflect. Comfort foods are welcome here (yes, the chocolate counts), journaling helps untangle busy feelings, and hydration keeps bloating and headaches away. Early nights and soft routines will carry you through.

Nourish your cycle 🥗

Small, kind food choices that make a real difference.

  • 🩸 Iron is your period hero. You lose iron when you bleed, so reach for spinach, lentils, beans, dates and raisins. Pair plant iron with vitamin C (a squeeze of lemon, an orange) so your body absorbs more of it.
  • 💧 Hydration, always. Aim for 6–8 glasses of water a day. Staying hydrated eases bloating, headaches and fatigue — and warm water or herbal teas can feel extra soothing on crampy days.
  • 🌰 Magnesium for cramps & calm. Bananas, nuts, seeds, dark chocolate and whole grains are rich in magnesium, which helps relax muscles and steady your mood.
  • 🥑 Good fats, happy hormones. Your hormones are built from fats — avocados, nuts, seeds, ghee and fatty fish give your body the raw materials it needs.
  • 🍓 Eat the rainbow in follicular & ovulation phases when energy is high, and lean into warm, grounding comfort foods in your luteal and menstrual phases.
  • ☕ Gentle swaps: too much caffeine and salt can worsen cramps, bloating and tender breasts — no need to ban them, just notice how you feel and adjust with kindness.

Move with your cycle 🧘

There's no single "right" workout — there's the right workout for today's phase.

  • 🌧 Menstrual (Day 1–5): Gentle is powerful. Restorative yoga, slow stretching, a soft walk, or full rest days. Movement can ease cramps — but only the kind that feels like a hug, not a punishment.
  • 🌱 Follicular (Day 6–14): Energy is rising — build strength, try new classes, dance, hike, lift a little heavier. Your body recovers faster in this phase, so it's a great time to challenge yourself.
  • 💕 Ovulation (Day 14–17): Peak power! High-energy workouts, group classes, running, HIIT — you may feel your strongest and most unstoppable now. Channel the main-character energy. ✨
  • 🌙 Luteal (Day 18–28): Wind down gradually — pilates, swimming, cycling at an easy pace, long walks and mobility work. In the last few days, trade intensity for softness and sleep.
  • 💗 Golden rule: tired is information, not failure. Listening to your body is the training plan.

Your first period guide 🎒

New to periods? Welcome — you're in safe, friendly company. 💗

  • 🌸 What is a period? About once a month, your uterus sheds its lining — that's the blood you see. It's a healthy, normal sign that your body is growing up and working exactly as it should. Most girls get their first period between ages 9 and 15.
  • 👀 Signs it might be coming: white or clear discharge, breast tenderness, mild tummy cramps, moodiness, or a few spots. Your first one might be light, brownish, and a surprise — that's completely normal.
  • 🎒 Keep in your bag: 2–3 pads in a small pouch, a spare pair of underwear, a small plastic bag for used products, tissues or wet wipes, and a little snack. Being prepared = feeling calm.
  • 🩸 What to do when it starts: Put on a pad (sticky side on your underwear, wings folded underneath), change it every 4–6 hours, wash your hands before and after, and wrap used pads before throwing them in a bin — never flush them. If it starts at school, tell a teacher or friend — every woman around you has been exactly here. 💕
  • 📅 Good to know: early cycles are often irregular — that's your body learning. Tracking them (hello, Tracker tab!) helps you spot your own pattern. See a doctor if periods are extremely painful, very heavy, or stop for months.
